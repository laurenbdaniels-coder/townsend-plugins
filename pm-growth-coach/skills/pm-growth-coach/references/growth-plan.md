# Growth Plan & Practice Journal — Templates

Two artifacts: the plan (rewritten each cycle) and the journal (append-only). The design principle is deliberate practice: small reps, on real work, with a feedback source, reviewed honestly. A plan without dated reps attached to actual work is a reading list.

---

## `01-growth-plan.md`

## TL;DR & So What

*Max 2 paragraphs: (1) the 1–2 focus dimensions and why they're the highest-leverage choice for the user's stated goal (name the tradeoff — what scored lower but matters less right now), (2) what a successful cycle looks like by the re-score date.*

### Goal (per focus dimension, max 2)

- **Dimension:** e.g., Structured discovery
- **From → to:** baseline anchor behavior → target anchor behavior, in words, not just numbers ("from 'conclusions don't cite counts' to 'every synthesis has sources and a disconfirming section'")
- **Target-anchor date:** realistic for anchor-level change — usually a quarter or more
- **This cycle's leading indicators (by the 4–6-week re-score):** the specific behaviors that should be visible in new work if the reps are landing — this is what the short-cycle re-score checks, not anchor movement
- **Why this one:** the leverage argument, one sentence

### Weekly reps

*Each rep: small enough to certainly happen, attached to real work happening anyway, with a named feedback source. No PM work happening yet (aspiring PM)? Attach reps to PM-shaped opportunities in your current role — a one-pager for a live internal decision, interviewing three internal users of a broken process, running a team retro — or a small live side project with real users; never purely hypothetical exercises. Examples of well-formed reps: "This week's customer call: past-behavior questions only; afterward, mark every question I asked as past/hypothetical and count." · "Add a non-goals section to the spec I'm writing Tuesday; get one engineer to react to it." · "Next status update: lead with the risk, path to green with an owner and date; note how the room responds."*

## `02-journal.md` (append-only)

*One dated entry per rep or notable moment. The lesson column is the point — activity without a lesson is exercise, not practice.*

```
## YYYY-MM-DD — [dimension]
Rep:          what I set out to do
What happened: specifics, not "fine" — include the uncomfortable parts
Evidence:     artifact/quote/reaction that shows it (link the file if in a workspace;
              describe colleagues' reactions without naming them unless this folder is verified private)
Lesson:       what I'd repeat or change — one or two sentences
Next rep:     carried into the plan
```

---

## Re-scoring rules (every 4–6 weeks)

- Same rubric, same evidence rules: **movement requires new evidence** — a rep log and changed artifacts, not elapsed time or effort expended.
- **Two horizons:** the 4–6-week re-score checks this cycle's leading indicators and records "trending toward [anchor]"; anchor-level score movement happens only when new evidence matches the higher anchor's behaviors — typically a quarter-plus. Pinning anchor jumps to short cycles produces either courtesy bumps or fake failure.
- **Scores can move down** when new evidence contradicts the baseline — frame it as calibration improving, not skill declining, and name which reading changed.
- **Third-party calibration:** each baseline and re-score includes at least one outside data point per focus dimension (a targeted question to a manager or peer, a verbatim reaction, a meeting observation). Stakeholder-management scores backed only by your own documents cap at Low confidence — that's the dimension self-assessment gets most wrong.
- Record the delta in `00-baseline.md` with a dated entry; celebrate what moved, concretely and specifically.
- **Two flat re-scores in a row = the plan is wrong, not the person.** Change one of: rep size (usually too big), feedback source (usually missing), or focus dimension (sometimes wrong leverage). Say this out loud — stalled learners quietly conclude they're the problem.
- When a target anchor is reached: either deepen (aim at the next anchor) or rotate to the next-leverage dimension — the user chooses, with a recommendation.

## Quality bar

- [ ] At most 2 focus dimensions, chosen by leverage with the tradeoff stated
- [ ] Goals written as behavior change (anchor → anchor), not just numbers
- [ ] Every rep is dated, real-work-attached, and has a feedback source
- [ ] Journal entries contain lessons, not just activity
- [ ] Re-scores cite new evidence; flat progress triggers a plan change
- [ ] Support-needed section honest (empty only if truly nothing is needed)
