# Goal Status Update — Template

The "where do we stand" statement for a goal, at every status cycle and whenever a color changes. The most stakeholder-read text a PM produces. Also cited by delivery-planning's weekly status headline — same color discipline there. *Solo founder: use the color definitions and the outcome rule; skip the ceremony.*

---

## Required construction

Open with one of: **"We are [green/yellow/red] because…"** / "We completed…" (completed goals only). One paragraph, two to four sentences, active voice, no bullets. No weasel words — the number or the specific impact, per `standards/evidence-rules.md`.

## Color definitions (colors follow definitions, not vibes)

- **Green** — on schedule to deliver original scope by the original date; for metric goals, on or ahead of the agreed ramp.
- **Yellow** — in jeopardy (date, scope, or ramp at risk) but recoverable. Yellow REQUIRES a realistic path to green. No credible path, no yellow.
- **Red** — will not meet the original commitment. Red requires the revised-date sentence: "There is no path to green for our original date of [date]. We are working toward a revised date of [date]."
- **Completed / Completed late / Did not meet** — original scope by original date / after it / won't be met (say why: environment change, strategy change, or execution miss).

## Skeleton by color

- **Green:** "We are green because [milestone hit or metric vs. ramp]." + supporting numbers in context. + what ships next, when.
- **Yellow:** "We are yellow because [specific miss with date/metric impact]." + root cause and customer/business impact. + "The path to green is…" with actions, named owners, dates, and the metric that confirms recovery.
- **Red:** "We are red because [the blocker]." + root cause with data. + the mandatory revised-date sentence.

## The outcome rule

Pair every delivery line with an outcome line: "we shipped X" AND "metric moved Y against target Z" — or, when genuinely too early, "we begin measuring on [date]." Shipping is not the goal; the goal is the goal. A goal with no outcome metric is the real status problem — flag it before polishing prose.

## Path-to-green quality bar

A path to green is a plan, not hope. "[Owner] ships the fallback API by August 15, restoring the signup ramp by September" passes; "we are working closely with partners to accelerate" fails. If no such plan can be written, the honest color is red.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Color honest per the definitions — would a skeptical stakeholder agree?
- [ ] Opens with the required construction
- [ ] Delivery line AND outcome line present (or an explicit measurement date)
- [ ] Yellow: path to green names actions, owners, dates — not adverbs
- [ ] Red: revised date stated in the mandatory sentence
- [ ] Any quiet scope-shrink keeping this green? Call it out as a scope change needing sign-off
- [ ] Numbers as sourced; TK for anything missing — never an invented figure
